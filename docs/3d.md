# The 3D worlds of the Gravity Battery lab

Written by the lead from `src/three`, for the reviewer and the film. Four worlds, 52 parts, every length from `src/three/dims.ts` (metres; each constant says `reported`, `derived` or `estimate`; what the physics needs lives in `src/sim/machine.ts` and is imported, never retyped). `src/three/index.ts` exports the four as `lazyModule`s, so nothing is built until the visitor goes there. The land, plants, buildings and sky of the `site` are in `docs/scenery.md`.

## One machine, one function

`buildMachine` (`rig/machine.ts`) makes the whole machine, in `site`, `tower` and `hoist`, at the same place: the tower stands at the origin of all three. A world only decides which groups it registers as parts. `site` bakes everything that stands still into one mesh per material and winds the rope coarser: the same triangles at the same places outside the winding, and fewer draw calls. `same.test.ts` holds the shared parts within 3 mm across the worlds. The `update` of the machine is the one place that reads the simulation, so the weight, the rope, the sheaves and the brakes do the same thing everywhere.

| File                       | What                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `rig/structure.ts`         | what stands still: pad, four legs, lattice, rails, buffers, ladder, ruler, head frame with its deck and roof. Every member ends on another one (tested)                            |
| `rig/weight.ts`            | what moves with the weight: carrier and sling, blocks, ties, shoes, safety gear, hook block. The load path is drawn link by link: sheaves, hook, crosshead, uprights, deck         |
| `rig/hoist.ts`             | the hoist on the maker's dimensions (S9 p. 1/55), in force order: real solids, so a section shows the gears, the rotor and the inside of the drum                                  |
| `rig/rope.ts`              | one rope: drum, fall 1, sheave A, fall 2, return sheave, fall 3, sheave B, fall 4, socket. Segments are aimed every frame between markers on their parts, in the exploded view too |
| `rig/rope-flows.ts`        | "Flows" in `tower` and `hoist`: the same three paths, read each frame from the ends of the falls, at 4 v, 2 v and 0                                                                |
| `rig/shapes.ts`, `look.ts` | the solids, from dimensions passed in; the materials per world, a smallest width on screen for the rope, the drum's helical groove                                                 |
| `powerroom/room.ts`        | the shed's shell: one function, closed in `site`, open towards the visitor in `power-room`                                                                                         |

Ids and FR/EN names: `docs/research.md` §4, `HOTSPOTS` in each `scene.ts`, the sheets in `src/data/parts`. A landscape to the kit is a world called `site` (far plane 4000 m). `tower` takes `near` 0.05 and `far` 140 m (kit 0.6.0), with the camera's limit at 72 m and fog from 86 to 135 m, behind the whole machine from that limit (tested); the floor fades by its own radius (`fadeFloor`), so that no hard line crosses a close view. `hoist` and `power-room` keep the default 0.01 and 60 m.

**Selecting a part** (kit 0.6.0). `FRAMING` in `tower/scene.ts`, `hoist/scene.ts` and `powerroom/scene.ts`: a `frame` for what its box does not say (a stretch of a leg, of the bracing, of a rail, of the ladder, of the ruler; the pad's corner; the module and not its rack, a device and not its conduit, the button and not its wire), and a `viewDir` for **every** part of these three worlds: without one the kit keeps the camera's direction, and the part before it decides what stands in front (review D43). The guides, the envelope and a part shown alone keep the whole part. `declutter` is on in the four worlds: with every label on, a name with no room folds into its dot.

**What rides on the weight.** The opening climb ends at the top after 5 minutes, and the weight stays there until evening, as the model has it: the machine draws what the state says, and the camera does the work. No direction serves the whole stroke (a strut or a diagonal crosses any line of sight at some height, and at the top the head frame's ring beam stands in front of everything seen from above; the kit's orbit stops at about 5° under the target). So what rides on the weight keeps a short list of directions, and `rig/sight.ts` takes the first with a clear line through the steel when the kit reads `viewDir`: a getter on the registry's entry, once per framing, none per frame, 5 to 75 ms on this Mac (the hoist's triangles are not tested). The distances it tests are the kit's for this view, read from the camera (`fitTangent`, `world.ts`): with a panel beside a narrow view the kit stands more than twice as far as in a wide one, and another strut is in the way (review D61). A `viewDir` set in the page replaces it, for a try.

- **The hook block**: at the top, 54 to 56 of 64 points by the recheck's count, selected and by the voice, at 1280, 768 and 390 (0 of 41 at the first review; at 768, 33 and 37 of 64 before the distances were the view's). From under the block near the top.
- **The rope** has two frames. Under the head frame, the falls just above the block. Above 0.9 of the stroke the falls have gone up into the ring beam, and all but a metre of the 84 m lies on the drum: it frames the drum (`ROPE_ON_DRUM`), from under the eaves, the roof out of most of the view: the turns seen are 293 × 48 px of a view 842 wide at 1280, 170 × 28 at 768, 173 × 28 at 390. The falls inside the beam show by their outline and the flows' dots only. In `tower`, where the drum is no part, what is wound on it belongs to the rope (it moves from one entry's `meshes` to the other's): outlined, picked and isolated with it. Its name's dot rides on fall 1 three metres above the block, then on the drum. To measure it, count the points in the frame: the falls inside the ring beam cannot be seen from anywhere, and half of each turn is behind the drum (the test counts the half that faces the camera).
- **The safety gear** stays the weak one: 18 to 22 of 45, inside the sling. X-ray or isolate show it.

**The ruler says what the model says.** `rig/ruler.ts`: metres every 5 m on the right, watt-hours every 200 Wh on the left, the top figure when it stands apart (1090 Wh), on plates at the strip's edges. Read from the state, drawn again when the mass or the stroke changes: every machine is drawn on this tower by its share of the stroke, so a ruler written once would lie. Stroke from `fallLength`; mass from `ghostTonnes / ghostTowers`, else `storedWh / z`, else the last one known (a weight at the bottom and too light for a night leaves nothing to read it from: kit request 25). Whole numbers only, no thousands mark, so nothing differs between the languages. Height unmarked, energy †, as this part's sheet writes them. Strokes as geometry, no texture: three draw calls, a few hundred triangles. The index on the carrier and the ruler's zero are both `RULER_ZERO`: 0 at the bottom, 20 m and 1090 Wh at the top.

Where the ruler is read: selecting it, or the voice, frames what can be read where the index is, a figure of each kind (`RulerMarks.reading`), from a direction chosen as for the weight. The weight stays at the top from the fifth minute, so that is where it matters: the two last figures sit just under their bands (`LAST_ROW_DROP`), because level with them the metres' plate stood behind the joint of the +X +Z leg (review D59).

**Names on narrow views** (`holdLabel`, `world.ts`, under 1180 px of page, and under 520 for what differs on a phone): the room's module, inverter and supply, the ruler and the ladder take a shorter name; the house's name reads to its left; the barn's dot moves to the ridge, and on a tablet the tower's down its shaft. The room's home comes from 26° (D35). A label's side and `declutter` for the main labels remain the kit's (requests 19, 23).

## What each world reads from the simulation

- **The machine**: `zFraction` (never `z`: a preset may have another stroke), `fallLength`, `blocks` and `halves`, `drumAngle`, `sheaveAngle`, `motorAngle`, `motorBlur`, `brakeReleased`, `safetyGearSet`, `mode`. Gears are drawn turning up to 4 rad/s and 14 teeth a second, then a blur disc takes over. The moves of the brakes are drawn larger than life (a few tenths of a millimetre in truth): they say that it happens, not how far.
- **`site`**: `hour` (sun, moon, stars, windows, the tower's lamp), `load`, `pv`, `gridPower`, `chain.meter`, `flowDirection`, `ghostTonnes`, `ghostBlocks`, `ghostTowers`, and through `params` the `wind`, `gateOpen` and `grid`, which the state does not hold (kit request 18).
- **`power-room`**: `flowDirection` and the powers for the flows; `dcVolts` for the link's lamp, which stays on while it holds a voltage; `chopperPower` for the coils; `soc` for the module's bars; `drive`, `alarms` for the lamps of the safety chain.

**The ghost stack** (`site/scene.ts`) is the comparator the owner chose: real blocks on the meadow, in stacks like the machine's, and lattice towers along the far side of the farm, as many as a night takes, the last one as tall as what is left of a tower. It is a translucent copper, a quantity and not a thing. A label stands over it, with it and only with it, and reads the state (« 128 t\*, 6,4 tours\*, pour 3 kWh\* »); clicking it opens the block yard's sheet.

**What belongs to no part.** The governor's rope and its lower sheave, and the motor's cable, are scenery in every world, so the registry does not hide them and they follow no part. `holdScenery` shows them only when every part is there and in its place: isolate or a guide's step hides them, the exploded view too. In `hoist`, where the rope is no part, it goes with them while a part is hidden, and stays exploded, aimed at its markers. A blur disc shows only while the part that holds it is shown (`machine.blurAllowed`).

## Weak points, known and open

- **Labels.** Measured with the recheck's script, on all four sides of the view and against the toolbar and the panel. Main labels at home, the tower at eight heights, with and without the voice's dock, French and English: no cut, none on another, at 1280 × 800, 768 × 1024, 390 × 844, 360 × 800, 1024 × 768, 600 × 800, 1112 × 834, 1100 × 800, 1180 × 820, 1194 × 834, 820 × 1180, 600 × 960, 430 × 932, 412 × 915, 1366 × 800, 1920 × 800 (at 1024 × 768 and 600 × 800 English before the last dot was moved). Every part selected, with the weight at the bottom: the same at 1280, 768, 390 and 360 in both languages, at 1024 and 600 in French. Dots and short names do this by hand, size by size: only the kit's `declutter` for the main labels can hold at a size nobody measured (request 23).
- **The safety gear.** See above. Look at it in motion, on the scenario « Survitesse ».
- **The ghost's label on a phone.** It says the mass and the towers, not the kWh: the whole does not fit to the right of its dot.
- **The farm on narrow screens.** The kit sets the distance by the width. The farm is held at 320 m, with the barn and the house just in the view.
- **The rope in the falls at the top.** Inside the ring beam nothing shows it but the outline and the flows, which draw through it. That is what such a machine looks like.
- **Close-ups at ×600.** A part on the weight is framed where it is when selected: at a fast clock it has moved away a moment later.

## Tests

`dims.test.ts`: the maker's page, what is imported from the physics, the levels adding up, the rope on the drum. `rig/machine.test.ts`: the rope's path, the sense of rotation, nothing floating, contacts measured by rays. `rig/same.test.ts`: the same machine in three worlds, every scenario and preset of the physics, the budgets. `rig/review.test.ts`: what the independent review found, held: nothing left standing under isolate or exploded, the roof with its posts, the groove, the rope clear of the guide, the fog, the farm on the ground, the rope's flows, the gears in section, the ghost's label; the block and the rope seen at every height, a direction for every part, the ruler's figures against the model at nine machines and its index, the paint line, the frames. `site/site.test.ts` and `powerroom/powerroom.test.ts`: the world's own rules (see `docs/scenery.md`).
