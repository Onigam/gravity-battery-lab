# Kit requests (what `@onigam/lab-kit` 0.5.1 lacks, from the physics and panel work of Gravity Battery Lab)

Numbered, one paragraph each. Nothing here was worked around by patching the kit; each item says what the lab did instead. Items 1 to 5 repeat Mill Lab's requests 7, 2, 3, 5 and 6: this lab is their next user.

1. **`sim/mech` into the kit (Mill's request 7).** The second user has arrived. `src/sim/mech.ts` here is a subset of `mill-lab/src/sim/mech.ts`, copied as it is: `rpmToRad`, `radToRpm`, `totalRatio`, `torque`, `stagesNeeded`, `driveTrain`, `Generator`, `generatorAt`, `matchSpeed`. A common `@onigam/lab-kit/sim/mech` removes the copy. While there: `backDriven(η) = 2 − 1/η` and a reeving helper (`reevingSumUp`, `reevingSumDown`, in `src/sim/reeving.ts`) would fit, and `driveTrain` would gain from a direction (going up a train, the torque rises through 1/η; the lab passes `1/η` as the efficiency).
2. **A fake DOM for panel tests (Mill's request 2).** `src/panels/test-dom.ts` is copied from Mill Lab, itself from Piggott. Ship it as `@onigam/lab-kit/testing`.
3. **`num()`, and units (Mill's request 3).** `num` is copied again, in `src/panels/strings.ts`. This lab also writes `powerText`, `energyText` and `durationText`, which pick W, kW, MW, or Wh to GWh, or s to days: every energy lab needs them.
4. **`section(title, …)` that can start closed (Mill's request 5).** The panel sets `.open = false` on what it gets back.
5. **A `readout` widget (Mill's request 6).** With a place for a mark: every number here carries `†` or `*`, which the lab appends to the unit.
6. **Charts: real logarithmic axes.** The ladder (mass against height, log-log, lines of equal energy, eleven machines) fakes them as Mill's chooser does: log10 values on linear axes, each line with its own stretch of a shared list of abscissas, a box chosen so that the kit's four ticks fall on whole powers (−3, 1, 5, 9). Please add a log scale and explicit ticks (Mill's request 4).
7. **Charts: named lines and markers that do not collide.** A line of equal energy has no label of its own: the lab puts a marker on each and reads its name there, which also draws a dot. Markers near each other overlap their labels (machines 4 to 8 on the ladder); the lab numbers them and writes the list under the chart. A `label` on a series at a chosen abscissa, and a marker option `{ dot: false }`, would do, and a simple collision step for labels.
8. **Charts: a zero line and an axis through zero.** The chart of what comes out goes below zero, where the whole lesson is: below the cut-in the machine pays. The kit draws the zero like any other grid line, and its four ticks at thirds force `−a, 0, a, 2a` (`axisThroughZero`). A heavier zero line, and ticks by value, would say it.
9. **A status line.** Each lab rebuilds "one message: a callout when it warns, a hint when it does not" (`renderStatus`). A small `status(text, tone)` in the kit's widgets would make them the same.
10. **A loss bar.** The chain's bar and its legend (`.losses`, `.loss-bar`, `.loss-legend`) have a stylesheet in the kit and no builder; each lab writes the same fifty lines (`renderChain`). This lab uses the same markup for its race bars too. A `lossBar(rows)` would hold both. The `.loss-bar` also clips very small segments; a smallest width would keep them visible.
11. **Time controls of a scenario.** A scenario here sets its own `timeScale` (a day at ×600, a fall at a slow motion of 1/20) through `params`, which the strip reflects at once. That works, and a note in `ui/lab.d.ts` saying that it is allowed would settle it.
12. **A clock with no date.** `strip.clock` wants a line and a sub-line; a time of day has only one. The lab returns an empty `sub`, and the strip keeps the room for it.
13. **Spoken guide cues for a scenario.** Foreseen by the research (`narration.onCue`, so that the weight moves while the narrator speaks): the engine's `applyScenario`, `applyPreset` and `set` are ready for it, no change is needed on this side.

## From the 3D of the same lab

14. **A far plane per world.** A world that is not called `site` gets a far plane of 60 m (`Stage.setActive`). The `tower` world is 30 m tall, seen from 40 to 50 m: it fits only because its paper fog closes at 59 m, and `maxDistance` is held at 52 m. A `SceneModule.far` (and `near`), or a far plane fitted to the parts, would free it. The same goes for "a landscape is a world called `site` or `intake`": a field on the `SceneModule` instead of the id.
15. **What X-ray is doing, in `ViewOpts`.** To keep what is inside a shell solid under X-ray (gears in the gearbox, rotor in the motor, cards in the cabinets), the world reads the display of every part each frame, counts the solid ones to guess whether a part is selected, and puts the solid material back on the meshes it wants solid. `ViewOpts.xray: 'shell' | 'selected' | null`, and an `inner` flag on a mesh that the registry would leave solid in `'shell'` mode, would replace all of it. The registry's `setDisplay` could also skip it.
16. **`mergeStatic` and `applyWear` into the kit.** `src/three/merge.ts`, `detail.ts`, `common.ts` (`FlowDots`, `paperFloor`) and `site/materials.ts`, `site/sky.ts`, part of `site/vegetation.ts` are copied from Mill Lab, as Mill's were from Pelton's. Three labs and more now carry them. `merge.ts` gained one option here (`bakeKey`: a group's baked materials kept apart from the others', so that a section clips only its own).
17. **A smallest width on screen for what is thin.** `rig/look.ts` has `patchMinWidth`, a vertex patch that keeps a rope 20 mm across at least a pixel wide from 90 m, where it would flicker: any lab with a rope, a wire or a cable needs it. In the kit, it would know the real height of the canvas (the lab assumes 800 px).
18. **`SceneModule.update` and the parameters.** `wind`, `gateOpen` and `grid` are in `params`, not in the state: the lab passes `() => sim.params` to `createSiteScene`, as Mill passes its ratios. An `update(state, dt, view, sim)` would settle it for every lab.
19. **Hotspots with a side.** At the overview of a tall, narrow machine, labels all extend to the right of their dot: eight names on the 5 m at the foot of a 28 m tower cannot be side by side (`labels: all`). A `side: 'left'` on a hotspot, or a simple collision step, would let them be.

## From the integration of the same lab

20. **The cartouche: « Projection européenne » touches the line beside it.** The kit writes it to the left of the title block, ending on the block's edge, and the lab's disclaimer starts on that edge: on every sheet the two run together (« européenneNE PAS CONSTRUIRE »). A gap of a few millimetres in the kit would do; nothing here works around it.
21. **The part sheet's tabs wrap at 1280 in French.** « Aperçu, Caractéristiques, Entretien, Conception » take two lines in the default panel: readable, and the same in every French lab, but the fourth stands alone on its line.

## From the fixes after the review

22. **A hotspot that follows the state.** The ghost stack of `site` needs a label that says what it is, reads the state, and shows only with the ghost. The kit reads a label's text and its visibility when the display changes, not every frame, and a hotspot needs a part. The world writes the DOM itself (`.hotspot__text`, `style.visibility`) from the `CSS2DObject` under its anchor. The same world sets `text-transform: none` on that one label, since the kit's capitals would misspell its units (« 128 T, 3 KWH »). A `Hotspot.label` that may be a function, a `Hotspot.visible()` and a way to keep a label's case would replace all of it. Kit 0.6.0 does not have them.

## From the review of 3 November

23. **`declutter` for the main labels too, and a label's side (request 19).** `declutter` only acts with every label on. The main labels can still lie on each other at a width nobody tried: the lab moved dots and shortened names (`holdLabel`, `world.ts`) until 1280, 768 and 390 were clean, and at 1024 the tower's and the barn's names still touch. A view that the dock raises flips which of two labels is above the other. A collision step on the labels shown, whatever the mode, and a `side: 'left'` that keeps the dot on the part (the lab sets `CSS2DObject.center.x = 1` for the house, which leaves the dot at the far end of the name), would replace all of it. D12, D47.
24. **A part's `viewDir` as a function, and an owner for a mesh.** What rides on the weight needs its direction worked out when it is framed: the lab defines a getter on the registry's entry (`askedWhenFramed`, `tower/scene.ts`). `PartFraming.viewDir?: Vector3 | (() => Vector3)` would say it. The same world moves the wound rope from one entry's `meshes` to another's by hand: `registry.add` could take extra meshes. And `SceneModule.update` does not say which part is selected, so a world cannot adapt what it shows to it. D42.
25. **The mass in the state (to the physicist, not the kit).** The ruler reads the mass through `ghostTonnes / ghostTowers` or `storedWh / z`. A load too light for a night with the weight at the bottom leaves nothing to read: the ruler keeps the last mass known until the weight moves. `GravityState.mass` would settle it in one line. The same goes for the machine's id, which only `site` gets through its `params` (request 18).
26. **A home for narrow views.** `frameAll` keeps the world's direction and fits the width, and only `site` and `intake` take an authored pose: a room is a strip across a tall view (D35). The world writes `home.position` every frame from the page's width. A `homeNarrow` on `SceneModule` would say it, and a `narrow` in `ViewOpts` would spare every lab its `clientWidth`.
27. **D46 and D48, for the record.** A drag that starts on another part loses the selected one, in the hoist; the first-visit bubble covers what it sits on. Both are the kit's, listed in `docs/qa.md`, nothing here works around them.

Answered by kit 0.6.0 and used in `src/three`: 14 in part (`near`, `far`; the rest stays open in the kit's list), 19 in part (`declutter`, for the collisions with every label on; a label's side stays open), the explode flag and the frame box per part that `docs/qa.md` asked of the lead. `merge.ts`, `common.ts` and the copied `detail.ts` were left as they are: moving to the kit's `applyWear` and `contactShadow` is a choice of its own (`lab-kit/docs/kit-0.6.0.md`).
